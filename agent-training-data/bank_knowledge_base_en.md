## Bank Overview
Bin Dowal Islamic Microfinance Bank is a modern national banking institution established in 2021 under an official license issued by the Central Bank of Yemen. It is one of the leading banks in providing Islamic financial and banking solutions for individuals and small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), promoting financial inclusion in Yemen.

- **Establishment Year:** 2021
- **Head Office:** Mukalla - Hadramout - Republic of Yemen
- **Network:** 35+ branches and 1700+ service points across the "Dowal Express" network.
- **Vision:** Leader in financial inclusion and providing innovative, efficient banking solutions to empower clients.
- **Mission:** Provide an integrated financial inclusion model focusing on social and economic empowerment of beneficiaries in accordance with Islamic Sharia principles.

- **Local & International Coverage:** Ability to issue guarantees in favor of entities inside or outside the country.

#### Requirements & Conditions:
- A formal request to issue the guarantee specifying the purpose, beneficiary, and amount.
- A copy of the tender invitation or relevant contract.
- An active bank account with available collaterals or approved facilities.
- Audited financial statements of the company (if an uncovered facility is requested).

#### Steps to Apply:

#### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs):
* **Q: What is the cash margin for a guarantee?**
  * A: It is a percentage of the guarantee amount frozen in the company's account as collateral, ranging from 10% to 100% based on credit assessment.

* **Q: Can a letter of guarantee be cancelled before its expiry?**
  * A: Yes, it can be cancelled if the original letter of guarantee is returned to the bank or a written release from the beneficiary is received.

* **Q: Are shipping and customs clearance guarantees available?**
  * A: Yes, we offer guarantees directed to customs and port authorities to facilitate goods clearance.

* **Q: What is the difference between a bid bond and a performance bond?**
  * A: A bid bond guarantees the company's seriousness in entering a tender, while a performance bond guarantees proper execution of the awarded contract.
